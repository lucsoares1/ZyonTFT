# Design System: Zyon TFT

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Composition Workbench"**

Zyon is a dense, quiet workspace for a player keeping a composition in view. Graphite surfaces and an olive navigation rail frame champion artwork; compact Manrope text keeps controls subordinate to the roster and board. Image crops carry the subject, while restrained gold establishes ownership and selection.

The signature relationship is visible in two places: a pinned roster and its matching shop slots. Mint communicates active status. Source labels and uncertainty remain visible beside the relevant controls, consistent with the product's commitment to showing where data comes from. This document records the implemented desktop interface and its responsive adaptation, rather than a marketing identity.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Graphite workspace, olive navigation, ivory text.
- Champion artwork in cropped collections, portraits, and hexagonal board cells.
- Gold connects selection, pinning, and matching shop badges.
- Thin separators and tonal panels establish structure.
- Compact controls, explicit source states, and restrained motion.

## Colors

Warm gold and olive sit within a dark graphite field; artwork supplies most of the chromatic variation. Frontmatter values are normative and are extracted from `src/styles.css` and the cost mapping in `src/App.tsx`.

### Primary
- **Brushed Gold:** selection checks, active tab underline, focus outline, and input caret. Pinned actions, filled badges, and match outlines use their recorded component variants.
- **Cost Gold:** the fifth cost tier; preserve its data meaning separately from roster membership.

### Secondary
- **Muted Mint:** active-state family. The live dot and active overlay control use closely related local shades; do not interpret an active indicator as detection certainty.
- **Cost Green, Blue, and Violet:** the second through fourth champion cost tiers. These frame portraits and shop cards as data encodings.

### Neutral
- **Graphite / Ivory / Muted:** workspace background, primary text, and supporting text.
- **Olive Sidebar:** persistent navigation surface. Active navigation uses a lighter olive fill and warm text.
- **Board / Shop:** green-leaning dark containers with thin tonal boundaries.
- **Input:** slightly lighter graphite with an explicit boundary and warmer focus stroke.
- **Cost Gray:** the first champion cost tier.

**The Linked Gold Rule.** Use the same visual family for roster ownership, the pinned action, and shop membership. Preserve cost borders underneath this relationship; cost and membership are separate signals.

**The State Mint Rule.** Use mint for active status, paired with text. Gold continues to represent selection even when the overlay is active.

## Typography

**Display and Body Font:** Manrope, with Arial and sans-serif fallbacks. Weights 400, 500, 600, 700, and 800 are locally imported in `src/main.tsx`. There is no separate display or monospace family.

**Character:** compact, clear, and minimally expressive. Larger headings use tight tracking; functional text stays in sentence case. Small uppercase labels in the build identify data sources, slots, and the brand rather than introducing decorative sections.

### Hierarchy
- **Headline:** main workspace heading; detail headings use the same size and weight with slightly different tracking. Responsive headings fall to 20–21px.
- **Title:** settings and help section headings.
- **Body:** explanatory settings text, with a maximum width of 58ch; help text reaches 65ch and uses a slightly looser line height.
- **Section:** board and roster headings; library and shop headings use 12px variants.
- **Navigation:** compact, medium-bold labels beside line icons.
- **Label:** action buttons; metadata commonly uses 9–10px.
- **Badge:** the native shop membership marker; preview sizes are surface-specific reductions, not a general text scale.

## Layout

The desktop shell fills the viewport and contains internal scrolling regions. At the standard desktop width, a 204px sidebar precedes a flexible main workspace. A 53px topbar, a padded heading, a split composition area, a shop preview, and a 29px statusbar form the vertical hierarchy. Main horizontal padding is 30px; composition columns are separated by 28px. The library is 246px wide, growing to 269px at 1500px. These are observed screen dimensions, not a requirement for every future surface.

Above 1050px, the overview places a fluid board beside a 151px roster column with a thin divider; the roster uses two columns. The board is a four-by-seven staggered hex grid. At 1051–1200px, side navigation, library, gutters, portraits, and board cells contract. At viewport heights of 850px or less, vertical spacing and art heights contract as well.

At 1050px or less, the page uses normal vertical flow, the navigation rail becomes 75px wide, and settings stack into one column. At 720px or less, the rail becomes 56px; the composition library becomes a horizontal strip above the detail; primary content gutters become 17px. Board and roster stack. The shop keeps five slots, with a 270px minimum strip and local horizontal overflow. Calibration changes from four columns to two.

The native overlay is a separate transparent layer with pointer events disabled. Its small summary panel is anchored at the upper right; membership badges are positioned from the calibrated shop rectangle, not from the editor grid.

## Elevation & Depth

Workspace depth comes from tonal layering, thin separators, and image contrast. Ordinary cards, controls, board, and shop containers do not use shadows. The toast uses a diffuse shadow (`0 10px 30px #0006`); the native overlay summary and screenshot proof summary use a softer compact shadow (`0 5px 20px #0004`). Art cards use a bottom gradient to retain readable names.

**The Quiet Surface Rule.** Keep workspace containers flat; reserve floating shadows for information displayed above the workspace or game image.

## Shapes

Controls have gently curved corners: small badges and buttons use the small radius, fields use the field radius, navigation uses the control radius, composition cards use the composition radius, and large board/shop containers use the container radius. Thin one-pixel borders do most of the edge work. Cost borders gain a three-pixel lower edge on shop cards. Circular status dots, selected check marks, and the hexagonal board are established exceptions to the rectangular container language.

## Components

### Buttons

Compact, icon-led actions use locally sized padding rather than a large universal button. Primary and secondary reader actions use the frontmatter variants; pinning has its own gold filled state and lighter hover. The unpinned action is outlined. Icon-only controls use 28px square hit areas in the library and 34px squares in window controls. Disabled buttons use 0.45 opacity and a not-allowed cursor.

All buttons transition background, text, and border color over 160ms with ease timing. Enabled hover text warms; enabled press reduces brightness to 0.95. Keyboard focus uses a two-pixel gold outline offset by four pixels. Do not remove that focus treatment when adding a variant.

### Inputs / Fields

Search fields combine a line icon and transparent input within a bounded dark container. Focus-within warms both border and outline. Name fields use a slightly greener fill. Calibration fields place the percent suffix beside a numeric input, preserving a visible group boundary. Native range and checkbox controls use gold accent color. Selects retain visible boundaries in settings.

### Navigation

The olive rail pairs line icons with compact labels. Active navigation has a filled lighter olive rectangle and warm text; hover is a modest tonal lift. Below 1051px labels disappear while the icon rail remains. Detail tabs use a gold two-pixel underline and no raised tab surface.

### Chips / Status

Source and example labels are small outlined rectangles; they carry provenance, not interaction. Live status combines a circular dot and text. The membership badge is a filled gold rectangle with an inline pin SVG and a short label. Its entrance moves upward by three pixels over 240ms while the gold settles. Reduced-motion preference removes animations and transitions.

### Cards / Containers

Composition cards combine cropped art, narrow captions, and a miniature portrait strip. Active cards receive a gold border and a warmer fill. Artwork starts subdued and brightens on hover; avoid replacing subject imagery with decorative gradients. Catalog cards use the same art-plus-caption relationship with a corner selection check. Board and shop containers use tonal green surfaces with no shadow.

### Roster and Shop Relationship

Roster portraits retain a cost-colored border and numeric cost marker. An edited selected portrait gains a gold outline and round check. The five shop cards preserve their cost-colored bottom edge; matches add a separate gold outline offset by three pixels and a badge below the card. Empty slots keep their shape with a dashed outline and explicit empty label. This preserves stable alignment when cards disappear.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** connect the roster and matching shop slots through gold ownership marks while preserving cost colors.
- **Do** pair active-state dots and source labels with readable text.
- **Do** retain local champion artwork, subdued crops, and clear name captions.
- **Do** preserve visible keyboard focus and reduced-motion behavior.
- **Do** keep the native overlay transparent and pass clicks through to the game.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use mint as the selected-roster color; preserve the implemented state distinction.
- **Don't** add floating shadows to ordinary workspace cards.
- **Don't** turn source or slot labels into decorative eyebrow headings.
- **Don't** promote the mobile board's tiny positional annotations or reduced preview badge text into a general-purpose type token.

Not canonized: the compact mobile view contains exceptionally small board annotations and preview badge text. These are recorded as surface-specific density limitations, not reusable readability targets; this documentation pass does not alter implementation.
